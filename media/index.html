The problem

The customer sees one brand. Your reports see six channels.

Customers meet a brand across CTV, search, social, creators, Amazon and retail before they buy. Each platform reports its own success. Some of those sales would have happened anyway.

01

Every channel claims the sale.

Platform reporting tells you what each channel wants credit for. It does not tell you which activity changed the outcome.

02

The dashboard gets fuller.

More signals, more models and more reports can still leave the central question unanswered: what should happen next?

03

The decision gets harder.

Growth depends on knowing what caused it, what constrained it, and where another pound will create the most commercial value.

The Inflexion model

Media is not a collection of channels. It is a decision loop.

Understand the commercial objective. Plan the mix. Activate against it. Measure what changed. Learn from the result. Reallocate the next pound.

Every campaign should leave the next decision better informed than the last. That is the difference between buying media and building a media system.

The proof

A platform number is not the same as a business result.

Separating attribution from incrementality takes experiments, econometrics and commercial data. The dashboard is where the question begins, not where it ends.

What did the media cause?

Control groups and test design help separate exposed demand from the outcome that would have happened without the campaign.

Where should the next pound go?

Response curves and reallocation scenarios show where additional investment is likely to create the most commercial impact.
