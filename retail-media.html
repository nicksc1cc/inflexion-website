01. The real mechanics

What the algorithm rewards — and what most retail media setups get wrong.

The retail-media algorithm determines position from a weighted set of signals. No retailer publishes the full formula, but the inputs are known from published documentation and empirical testing: relevance (how well the product matches the search term), conversion rate (how often the page converts), click-through rate (how often the listing earns a click when shown), price competitiveness (relative to alternatives shown on the same page), review velocity and sentiment (recency and quality of customer reviews), and inventory depth (availability and delivery speed).

The most common mistake is treating retail media as a pure media buy: set a budget, bid on terms, optimise bids. That approach works only when the non-media signals are already competitive. If conversion rate, reviews or product data are weak, additional spend buys more of the same position. The constraint is seldom the bid.

We separate paid position from organic position in our reporting. A share of shelf number that hides how much was bought is not a measurement — it is a media invoice.

02. The platforms

Where we work and how.

Each platform has its own signal weighting and ad product structure. A strategy that works on Amazon does not transfer directly to Instacart, and neither works the same as Walmart Connect. We tailor the approach per platform and report them separately.

Amazon
Sponsored Products, Brands, Display and DSP.
Run as one campaign system, not four separate products. Amazon rewards product data completeness, conversion history and inventory depth alongside bid. We optimise all four, not just the bid.
Walmart
Walmart Connect.
Different signal weighting: Walmart places heavier emphasis on omnichannel sales data and in-store availability. The ad products are less mature but growing fast. Early movers have a structural advantage.
Instacart
CPG-focused retail media.
Heavily weighted toward category relevance and promotional history. Shelf position on Instacart behaves differently from Amazon because the buying intent is different — immediate delivery, not catalogue browsing.
Off-platform
Criteo, The Trade Desk, retail data partnerships.
The paid and social spend that drives people into the retail algorithm. We plan this alongside the retail media, not separately, because the traffic quality entering the funnel affects the algorithm's read of your product.
03. The audit

What we actually check.

A full retail media audit covers every signal the algorithm reads — not just the bid. Here is what we look at on every engagement.

01
Product data completeness.
Titles, bullet points, images, A+ content, attributes, backend search terms. The algorithm reads this before it reads the bid. Incomplete product data is the most common constraint we find.
02
Review velocity and sentiment.
How quickly reviews accumulate and what they say. The algorithm weights recency and sentiment alongside volume. A product with 500 old reviews can be outperformed by a product with 50 recent positive ones.
03
Conversion rate by traffic source.
Organic, sponsored, external — we track conversion separately for each. A blended number hides whether the problem is product or placement.
04
Price and promotion competitiveness.
Relative to the alternatives shown on the same search results page and product detail page. We benchmark pricing, coupon depth and deal timing against category competitors.
05
Inventory depth and fulfilment.
Stock-outs, shipping speed and fulfilment method all feed the algorithm. A product that cannot ship Prime loses position even if everything else is optimised.
06
Paid position vs organic position.
We separate the two in every report. If position improves but conversion does not follow, we flag it rather than present it as success.

06. What we won't tell you

The honest limitations, up front.

01
More spend will not fix bad product data.
If titles, images, attributes and reviews are not feeding the algorithm properly, additional budget buys more of the same position. The bid is not the constraint.
02
Rank improvements do not always convert.
We report rank and conversion separately so you can see which is improving and which is not. Presenting only the flattering number is a common agency practice we avoid.
03
Category dynamics cap what is achievable.
In some categories the top position is defended by spend levels that make chasing it a negative-ROI decision. We tell you this before you commit budget, not after.
04
The algorithm changes without notice.
Amazon and Walmart update their ranking signals regularly. What worked six months ago may not work today. We track signal changes continuously and adjust before the data shows a problem.
