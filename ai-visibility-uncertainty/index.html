Your AI visibility score is probably too precise.

On this page

A visibility score that moves from 42.1 to 44.8 may look scientific. Without repeated sampling, it may be decoration.

The noise is not a footnote

Ask the same question to an AI search engine twice and the answer may change. The wording can shift. The sources can shift. A brand named in the first response may disappear from the second.

That makes a single visibility score a weak measurement. It may be directionally useful, but it cannot tell you whether a two-point movement is real, random variation, a retrieval change, or a different answer assembled from the same underlying evidence.

An August 2026 paper by Ronald Sielinski makes the point directly. Citation visibility should be treated as a sample estimate of an underlying response distribution, not as a fixed property of a domain. Repeated sampling across Perplexity, OpenAI Search and Gemini found substantial variation over both daily and ten-minute intervals.

In one high-frequency test, Gemini showed citation-share movement in short intervals even though the underlying web content could not have changed. That is the distinction a dashboard needs to preserve: platform selection noise is not a change in brand authority.

Why one run fails

A dashboard that runs each prompt once has a clean interface and a noisy denominator. If the prompt set contains 100 questions, the result looks statistically substantial. It is not, if each question has only one observation.

Single runs create three common errors:

  • A competitor appears to overtake you because its name happened to be retrieved that morning.
  • A source appears to disappear because the model selected a different evidence path.
  • A campaign gets credit for a change that would have happened without it.

These errors matter commercially. Teams reallocate content and media budgets based on changes that are smaller than the measurement noise.

Sampling the answer

There is no single correct sampling design. The right one depends on the decision the measurement needs to support. But the design should be visible.

Use a fixed prompt set

Keep the questions stable long enough to see a trend. Split them by intent: category, comparison, alternative, problem and brand. If the denominator changes each month, the score is partly measuring the prompt editor.

Repeat the important prompts

Run buyer-critical prompts more than once per collection. Three runs per engine is a reasonable starting point for a small programme. More is better when the decision carries a high cost.

Keep the engine labels clear

ChatGPT with web search, Perplexity, Gemini and Claude do not represent one system. Report them separately before creating any composite view. A combined score can hide a material difference between engines.

Store the raw responses

Keep the response, citations, timestamp, model or search mode, prompt version and classification decision. Without the raw answer, a score cannot be audited later.

What to report

Report a rate, a range and a trend.

  • Mention rate: the share of sampled answers that name the brand.
  • Recommendation rate: the share that actively recommends the brand rather than merely naming it.
  • Citation rate: the share that links to the brand's own domain or a third-party source.
  • Engine coverage: where the brand appears and where it does not.
  • Uncertainty: a confidence interval or a simple repeat-run range.

Different vendors use different weights and definitions. The IAB noted in its August 2026 measurement playbook that more than 20 companies were selling AI visibility measurement with methodologies that can produce different answers for the same brand. Its proposed four-part view separates Presence, Prominence, Portrayal and Persuasion. No standard score exists yet. That is a reason to publish the method, not to hide it behind a single number.

The decision rule

Only act on a movement that clears the noise floor.

For a small team, the practical rule can be simple: repeat the collection, compare the same prompt and engine, and ask whether the movement appears across enough prompts to change the conclusion. If it appears in one answer, record it. If it appears across repeated samples, investigate it. If it persists across collections, act on it.

This slows the reporting cycle slightly. It also reduces the chance of rewriting a page because one model produced a different sentence.

AI visibility measurement is still young. The useful advantage will not come from claiming a perfect score. It will come from showing what the number includes, what it leaves out, and whether the movement is large enough to support a decision.
