The gap

The ambition is there. The architecture isn't.

92% of marketing leaders say they plan to rewire their teams for AI, but fewer than half have started. The gap is not willingness. It is knowing what to do, in what order, and how to make it stick.

The problem is structural. Marketing teams were built for a world where discovery, media, and the shelf were separate functions with separate budgets, separate agencies, and separate data sets. AI reads them all as one signal. The operating model has not caught up.

We help close that gap. Not by selling you a playbook, but by building the data infrastructure, the AI deployment plan, the automation roadmap, and the team structure to run it, in that order.

Maturity model

Three stages. Most teams are in stage one.

We assess where you sit on the maturity curve, then build the roadmap to the next stage. The goal is not to leap to stage three overnight. It is to make consistent progress that compounds.

Stage A

Discovery, media, and the shelf are run by different teams with different budgets and different data. Reporting is manual. Decisions are made on lagging indicators. Automation is limited to basic bid management. Most brands are here.

Stage B

Data is shared across functions. Common KPIs exist. Automation handles routine reporting and optimisation. The team is beginning to connect discovery and shelf signals. AI is used for content generation and basic analysis.

Stage C

One team runs discovery and the shelf with a single data layer. AI drives content creation, media allocation, citation work, and personalisation. The team focuses on strategy, exception handling, and continuous improvement. The operating model is sustainable.

The maturity model, applied

Where most teams sit, and where they go next.

The A-to-C maturity spectrum is not a theoretical model. It is based on patterns we see across brands that have attempted AI restructuring. Most brands are at A: siloed teams, manual processes, separate data sets. A minority reach B, where shared data and some automation exist. Almost none are at C.

Why this matters now

The team structure that worked in 2020 is already obsolete.

Execution is no longer the bottleneck

AI tools handle content creation, bid management, reporting and creative personalisation. The bottleneck is now strategic: what to prioritise, how to interpret the data, and when human judgement must override the machine.

Separate teams for discovery, media and the shelf hide the compounding effects

AI reads discovery signals, retail media signals and Amazon signals as one picture. The team structure should reflect that. Most brands still organise around channels that no longer exist as separate consumer experiences.

The operating model determines what is possible

The best AI deployment plan fails if the team cannot execute it. We design the structure, roles and governance first, then select the technology. Technology without operating-model change is the most expensive way to stay where you are.

Limitation: organisational change requires client leadership buy-in. We can design the model; the client must sponsor the transition.
