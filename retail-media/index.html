The real mechanics

What the algorithm rewards, and what most retail media setups get wrong.

The retail-media algorithm determines position from a weighted set of signals. No retailer publishes the full formula, but the inputs are known from published documentation and empirical testing: relevance (how well the product matches the search term), conversion rate (how often the page converts), click-through rate (how often the listing earns a click when shown), price competitiveness (relative to alternatives shown on the same page), review velocity and sentiment (recency and quality of customer reviews), and inventory depth (availability and delivery speed).

The most common mistake is treating retail media as a pure media buy: set a budget, bid on terms, adjust bids. That approach works only when the non-media signals are already competitive. If conversion rate, reviews or product data are weak, additional spend buys more of the same position. The constraint is seldom the bid.

We separate paid position from organic position in our reporting. A share-of-shelf number that hides how much was bought does not measure performance; it reports media spend.

The platforms

Where we work and how.

Each platform has its own signal weighting and ad product structure. A strategy that works on Amazon does not transfer directly to Instacart, and neither works the same as Walmart Connect. We tailor the approach per platform and report them separately.

Amazon
Sponsored Products, Brands, Display and DSP.
Run as one campaign system, not four separate products. Amazon rewards product data completeness, conversion history and inventory depth alongside bid. We improve all four, not just the bid.
Walmart
Walmart Connect.
Different signal weighting: Walmart places heavier emphasis on omnichannel sales data and in-store availability. The ad products are less mature but growing fast. Early adoption may provide an advantage, but the effect should be measured.
Instacart
CPG-focused retail media.
Heavily weighted toward category relevance and promotional history. Shelf position on Instacart behaves differently from Amazon because the buying intent is different: immediate delivery rather than catalogue browsing.
Off-platform
Criteo, The Trade Desk, retail data partnerships.
Off-platform paid and social activity drives traffic into the retail algorithm. We plan this alongside the retail media, not separately, because the traffic quality entering the funnel affects the algorithm's read of your product.
The audit

What we actually check.

A full retail media audit looks beyond the bid. These are the signals we check on each engagement.

01
Product data completeness.
Titles, bullet points, images, A+ content, attributes, backend search terms. The algorithm reads this before it reads the bid. Incomplete product data is the most common constraint we find.
02
Review velocity and sentiment.
How quickly reviews accumulate and what they say. The algorithm weights recency and sentiment alongside volume. A product with 500 old reviews can be outperformed by a product with 50 recent positive ones.
03
Conversion rate by traffic source.
We track organic, sponsored and external conversion separately. A blended number hides whether the problem is product or placement.
04
Price and promotion competitiveness.
Relative to the alternatives shown on the same search results page and product detail page. We benchmark pricing, coupon depth and deal timing against category competitors.
05
Inventory depth and fulfilment.
Stock-outs, shipping speed and fulfilment method all feed the algorithm. A product that cannot ship Prime loses position even if everything else is improved.
06
Paid position vs organic position.
We separate the two in every report. If position improves but conversion does not follow, we flag it rather than present it as success.
The approach

Map the signals, then allocate the budget.

The starting point on every engagement is a full shelf baseline: paid and organic positions separated, product signals audited, competitive landscape documented. The baseline determines the order of work. In most cases, product data fixes come first, review velocity improvements second, and media allocation third. The bid is rarely the constraint.

We report rank and conversion separately so you can see which is improving and which is not. We tell you before you commit budget if a category's top position is defended by spend levels that make chasing it a negative-ROI decision. The algorithm changes without notice, and we adjust before the data shows a problem.

Limitation: we cannot control retailer algorithm changes. When the platform shifts weighting, position can move overnight. We flag platform changes alongside the data so you know whether movement is in your positioning or in the algorithm.

Amazon / Rufus Sponsored Prompts

Rufus and the sponsored prompt evolution.

Amazon Rufus has evolved from a conversational shopping assistant to an ad platform. Sponsored prompts let brands appear in Rufus responses: product-level prompts trigger on specific ASIN queries, brand-level prompts fire on category or need-state searches.

The mechanic shifts retail media from keyword targeting to intent understanding. Rufus interprets the shopper's question, not their search string. Brands optimised for conversational discovery win placement ahead of those relying on bid alone.
