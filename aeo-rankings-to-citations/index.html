AEO: the shift from keyword rankings to citation tracking

Search is no longer about ranking for keywords. It is about being cited in AI answers. Here is how the measurement model is changing.

19 September 2026 · 10 min read · Inflexion journal
On this pageThe old modelThe new modelWhat measurement looks like nowDifferent engines, different signalsWhat this means for your strategy

For two decades, search reporting reduced discovery to a position, a visit and a conversion. AI answers add another question: which sources does the system use when it explains a choice?

SEO still drives valuable traffic. The change is that some discovery now happens inside an answer, before a searcher reaches a results page.

Semrush projects that LLM referral traffic will overtake traditional Google search by the end of 2027. In the past three months alone, LLM referrals grew 800 percent year over year. Google AI Overviews now appear on billions of searches monthly, covering at least 13 percent of all SERPs. The surface has changed. Reporting has not fully caught up.

The old model measured the result page

The old question was: where do we rank for this keyword? The emerging question is: does the model cite us when someone asks the underlying question?

Those outcomes can diverge. A page in position three may be missing from the answer to the same query. AI Overviews and chatbots do not simply reproduce the top ten results. They retrieve from a wider index, weigh authority differently and favour pages that expose a clear answer.

That makes position an incomplete proxy. A useful measurement system records whether the brand appears, where it appears in the answer and how the answer describes it.

The signals behind a citation

Traditional SEO reporting focuses on rankings, backlinks, page speed and crawlability. Citation tracking adds a different set of observations.

Authority includes the sources that mention or explain the brand. Backlinks still matter, but unlinked mentions across Reddit, Wikipedia, YouTube, industry forums and news outlets can also provide context. The model reads the surrounding text, not just the hyperlink.

Relevance comes from answering the question directly. A clear explanation near the start of a page gives a retrieval system more to work with than keyword-heavy copy that delays the answer.

Clarity is partly structural. Headings, schema markup, FAQ format, quotes and statistics make claims easier to extract. Pages with quotes or statistics have been shown to have 30 to 40 percent higher visibility in AI answers.

Freshness is another input. Update statistics, screenshots and examples, remove references to outdated tools and add sections when the subject changes. An old page can remain technically sound while becoming a poor answer source.

Measure share of answer, not just rank

The old dashboard showed keyword rankings, organic traffic and click-through rate. Keep those measures, but add a fixed prompt set and record what each engine returns.

Share of answer is the proportion of defined buyer prompts, across a defined set of models, where the brand appears in the generated answer. Record position as well. Being the first recommendation is different from appearing in a list of three.

Citation sources show where the answer got its evidence. Trace competitor mentions to independent reviews, comparison tables, analyst reports and forum discussions. That source map points to the credibility work a content calendar may miss.

Sentiment and framing describe how the answer positions the brand. A model calling a company "a leading provider" is doing something different from one saying it "also offers" the service.

Cross-model consistency prevents one platform from standing in for all of them. A brand cited in Claude can be absent from GPT. The 11 percent domain overlap between ChatGPT and Perplexity proves that the source sets differ.

Different engines use different evidence

ChatGPT favours credible sources with clear entity signals. Comparison content has a 95 percent citation rate. Documentation and resources hit 86 percent.

Google Gemini draws heavily from pages that already perform well organically, typically within the top 20 results. Blog content performs particularly well in AI Overviews at 42 percent citation rate.

Perplexity cites well-researched content that combines multiple perspectives. Academic and data-driven sources perform well. Blog posts and informative articles hit 80 percent citation rate.

Copilot favours pages that state what a product or service does, include pricing or comparison information and show strong brand signals.

These patterns are useful starting points, not permanent rules. Choose the engines your buyers use, then test the prompts that matter to them.

Build the baseline before changing the plan

On Marketing's October 2025 survey found that 37 percent of marketing teams were already actively optimising for AI search. Another 30 percent were exploring it. Eighty percent planned to have AI search strategies in place within a year.

Start with 5 to 10 high-priority buyer questions. Test them across ChatGPT, Gemini, Perplexity and Google AI Overviews. Record where your brand appears, where competitors appear and which sources support each answer.

Then build the operating system around that baseline: prompt mapping, citation tracking, source authority mapping and content designed for extraction. Review the same prompt set each month so the movement reflects visibility, not a new questionnaire.

AEO does not replace SEO. Strong SEO supplies useful pages to AI systems, but rankings alone no longer describe the whole discovery journey. The teams that add citation evidence to the dashboard can see what their buyers are being told, not just where a page appears.
