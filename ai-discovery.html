The AI visibility stack
01.The real mechanics

How AI models decide who to cite — and what that means for your content strategy.

When a user asks a model a question, the model does not search Google and read the top result. It generates an answer from its training data, then supplements that answer by retrieving from sources it has been trained to trust. Models rewards four things: authority (how often a source is cited by other sources the model trusts), relevance (whether the content directly answers the question being asked), clarity (whether the information is structured so the model can parse it), and freshness (how recent the content is).

There is no position one through ten. There is being in the answer and not being in the answer. Brands that rank first on Google for a query are often absent from the AI answer to the same query, because models and search engines weigh signals differently. We have measured this gap across dozens of categories. It is larger than most marketing teams realise.

This is why traditional SEO — built around keyword ranking, backlink volume and click-through rate — does not translate directly to AI citation. The work is adjacent but distinct. The skills overlap but the output metric is different. Most agencies selling "AEO" today are selling rebadged SEO. The actual work is about being the clearest, most authoritative source on a specific question, not about filling a keyword gap.

02.The audit

What we actually check when we check your AI visibility.

Nobody can plan this without a baseline, and almost nobody has one. The diagnostic is deliberately specified tightly enough that you could commission it from someone else. What you won't find elsewhere is the operational reading of the output and the plan that follows from it.

01
Prompt set construction.
We build a set of buyer questions specific to your category, covering the arc from broad category awareness through to comparison and purchase-intent queries. We exclude brand-name searches: nobody discovers you by typing your name. The prompt set is published to you and fixed for the duration of the engagement.
02
Cross-model testing.
The same prompt set run across four to six models, depending on what your buyers actually use. Models disagree more than expected: a brand well-cited in Claude can be absent from GPT, and neither pattern predicts Gemini or Perplexity. Testing one model gives a misleading picture.
03
Source mapping.
When a model names a competitor, we trace what it drew from. This reveals which sources carry authority in your category — independent reviews, comparison tables, analyst reports, forum discussions. The source map is often the most actionable finding in the audit, because it tells you exactly where to build credibility.
04
Gap analysis and prioritisation.
The baseline tells you two things: where you stand today, and what the category authority landscape looks like. The prioritised plan identifies which gaps are winnable within a quarter, which require longer investment, and which are structural constraints that cannot be engineered around.

The output is a baseline document, not a pitch. You keep it whether or not you work with us.

04.How we measure it

Share of answer, tracked. Not traffic, not rankings.

The metric
Share of answer.
The proportion of a defined buyer prompt set, across a defined set of models, in which your brand appears in the generated answer. Position within the answer is recorded — being named as the first recommendation is different from being mentioned in a list of three.
The integrity
The prompt set is fixed and published.
We do not change the prompts without telling you and re-baselining. Changing the questions changes the number, and we want the number to mean something.
The caveat
This is not revenue.
It is a leading indicator. Treat it the way share of search has been treated — an early signal that correlates with commercial outcomes, not a substitute for measuring them.

06.What we won't tell you it does

The honest limitations, up front.

01
It will not work in a month.
Models re-crawl and re-weight sources on their own schedule. Expect first meaningful movement around month three, and treat anyone promising faster with justified suspicion.
02
It will not fix weak positioning.
Models cite sources that answer a question well. If the underlying product, service or positioning is poorly defined, optimising the content around it will not rescue the narrative.
03
It is not permanent.
Models change, sources shift, competitors optimise. This is a tracked programme, not a project with an end date. The baseline should be re-run quarterly as a minimum.
