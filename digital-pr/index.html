Why engines trust earned media

The model has a credibility problem. Earned media is one signal that can help address it.

When an AI engine decides which brand to recommend, every brand's own website says the same thing: "we are excellent." Self-published content may provide less independent corroboration. Editorial coverage carries something brands cannot manufacture: an independent party chose to write about you. That signal maps directly to how retrieval systems and training corpora weight sources.

01
Trust calibration.
Models weight editorial sources as corroboration and promotional domains as claims. A mention in a trade publication with no byline link still reinforces entity recognition more than a perfectly improved product page. The model may use signals associated with the source and its content, not only the URL.
02
Entity reinforcement.
Consistent third-party descriptions of what your company does build the entity graph the model relies on. Every mention that correctly names your product category, use case, and differentiators feeds the same entity signal. Conflicting descriptions confuse the model and reduce citation probability.
03
Citation velocity.
How quickly new earned media appears in model outputs is a leading indicator of source authority. Brands whose coverage is reflected within days have stronger entity signals than brands whose coverage takes weeks to surface, if it surfaces at all.
04
The 2% rule.
Only 2% of journalists on standard PR pitching lists overlap with the outlets AI models actually cite. Traditional PR targets the wrong people. Citation-weighted PR starts from the model's source map, not a media database subscription. The remaining contacts may be less relevant to citation-focused outreach.

Phase 1
Source mapping
We run your priority buyer prompts across 6 models and record every source cited: every domain, every outlet, every forum. The result is a ranked list of publications that carry weight in your category. This is your targeting list.
Phase 2
Entity building
Wikipedia, Crunchbase, G2, industry directories, analyst listings: the sources models use to answer "who is company X." We audit every reference source, fix inconsistencies, and build a complete entity footprint before scaling outreach.
Phase 3
Earned coverage
We target the specific outlets models cite most in your category, not the outlets in a generic media database. A mention in a specialist trade publication that AI cites more often may be more relevant than several generic mentions from a syndicated wire.
Phase 4
Community presence
Reddit, Quora, YouTube, Stack Exchange, niche forums: community content is a growing share of AI citations. We help develop a presence in the platforms your category's buyers use, by contributing useful answers where appropriate.

Measurement in practice

Baseline. Deploy. Track. Refine.

The effect of earned media on AI citation cannot be measured in real time. Models re-crawl on their own schedules, and a mention published today may take weeks to appear in answers. We set expectations at month three and track from there. The shift is measured by running the same prompt set across six models every month and comparing citation volume changes with specific placements.

Single placements rarely move the number alone. The effect compounds across consistent coverage in the outlets the model trusts. Earning coverage there is not a quick fix. The outlets AI models cite form a relatively narrow set, and existing traditional PR contacts rarely translate. We are honest about that from the start. Map your citation sources →

Limitation: citation lag varies by model and source. A mention in a high-authority outlet may appear in days; the same mention in a lower-authority outlet may never appear. We report lag alongside volume so the timeline is visible.
