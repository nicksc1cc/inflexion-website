01. The methodology

Same questions. Same models. Comparable answers.

Measurement that changes the questions every quarter is not measurement — it's a mood ring. Our methodology is fixed, auditable, and built to be re-run by anyone.

01
Fixed prompt set.
We build a set of buyer questions specific to your category, covering the range from broad category questions through to comparison and purchase-intent queries. The set is fixed and published to you at the start. Changing the questions changes the number, so we don't change them without re-baselining.
02
Cross-model testing.
The same prompt set run across the models your buyers actually use — because models disagree with each other more than people expect. A brand can be well cited in one and absent from another. We test across ChatGPT, Gemini, Perplexity, Copilot, and category-relevant vertical models.
03
Source mapping.
When a model does name a brand, we trace what it drew from — owned content, earned media, review platforms, forums, structured data. That tells you which sources carry authority in your category, which is usually the most actionable finding in the whole audit.

The prompt set is re-run on a fixed monthly schedule. Every run is date-stamped, model-versioned, and stored so you can compare apples to apples across quarters.

02. What we measure

Four signals. One composite picture.

Headline share of answer is the composite. Each component tells you something different about how you're being surfaced, cited, and positioned.

01
Share of answer.
The proportion of prompts in which your brand appears in the model output. This is the headline metric. It tells you whether you're visible at all. The baseline is always zero until you measure it.
02
Position within the answer.
Being mentioned in passing and being named as the recommendation are fundamentally different outcomes. We track where in the output the brand appears — first recommendation, middle of a list, or a passing reference — because position correlates strongly with click-through and consideration.
03
Citation velocity.
How quickly new earned media and owned content are reflected in model outputs. Some brands take weeks for a press release to appear in citations. Others are reflected within days. Velocity is a leading indicator of source authority.
04
Sentiment & framing.
Whether the model presents your brand neutrally, favourably, or with caveats. Models don't express opinion — but they do frame. A brand described as "a leading provider" competes differently from one described as "also offers."
03. Competitive intelligence

Your share means nothing without the market.

Share of answer is a relative number. Knowing yours without knowing your competitors' is like knowing your position on a map without knowing where anyone else is standing.

01
Share of voice (SOV).
Each competitor's share of the total brand citations across the prompt set. Who owns the answer today, who is gaining, and who is being displaced. This is the competitive baseline that most brands cannot produce because they aren't running the same prompt set against their competitors.
02
Gap analysis.
Where competitors appear and you don't — by question, by model, by source type. The gaps tell you what to build, what to earn, and what to fix. They are the most actionable output of the entire measurement system.
03
Benchmarking.
How your category's discovery landscape changes over time. We track the average number of brands cited per prompt, the stability of citations across runs, and which source types are gaining or losing weight in model outputs.
04. Framework applied

From raw data to running decisions.

Measurement is useless without a framework that turns it into action. Here is how the data flows from collection through to the decisions it informs.

01
Collect
Fixed prompt set run across models on a fixed schedule. Raw outputs stored, versioned, auditable.
02
Score
Each brand's presence scored by occurrence, position, source type, and sentiment. Weighted by model share of use.
03
Surface
Gaps surfaced by question, model, and competitor. No dashboard that hides the bad news behind averages.
04
Prioritise
Gaps triaged by winability and commercial impact. The question with the highest combined score gets the work.
05
Track
Month-over-month change in share of answer and share of shelf. If the number isn't moving, we change what we're doing.

This is the same framework we apply to every engagement. It means the work is comparable across clients and across time.

05. Honest limitations

What this number does not tell you.

Every measurement system has boundaries. Ours does too. We set them out here because the number is only useful if you know what it excludes.

01
It isn't revenue.
Share of answer is a leading indicator, not a replacement for commercial measurement. It correlates with market share over time — it does not equal it. Treat it the way share of search has been treated: an early signal that earns you the budget to invest further.
02
Prompt coverage isn't universal.
No fixed prompt set captures every question a buyer might ask. We aim for representative coverage of the category's buying journey, but a prompt set is a sample, not a census. We tell you what's in and what's out, and we don't change it without warning.
03
Models change without notice.
When a model updates, your share of answer can shift overnight. We flag model-version changes alongside the data so you know whether the movement is in your positioning or in the algorithm. We cannot control the models' release schedules, only the transparency with which we report them.
04
Sentiment analysis is directional.
We categorise framing as positive, neutral, or negative based on structured criteria. But model outputs are probabilistic and framing can be subtle. We do not present sentiment scoring as precise — we present it as directional, with the raw output available for your own review.

We report these limitations alongside every measurement output. If you see a number from us, you will always also see its caveats.
