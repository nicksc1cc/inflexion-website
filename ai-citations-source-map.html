AI Discovery / Source Strategy

AI citations come from places your media plan does not control.

19 SEPTEMBER 2026 · 8 MIN READ · INFLEXION JOURNAL

The question is no longer only which page a model retrieves. It is which sources make the answer believable.

The source map is more important than the content calendar

Most AI visibility programmes begin with a question set and end with a list of pages to publish. That is backwards. Before deciding what to write, find out which domains answer engines already cite for the questions that matter.

Recent source analyses repeatedly surface Reddit, Wikipedia, YouTube, LinkedIn, news sites and specialist reviews. The exact ranking changes by engine and category. The stable finding is that a brand's own website is only one part of the evidence available to a model.

Peec AI's analysis of 30 million sources found Reddit, YouTube, LinkedIn, Wikipedia and Forbes among the most-cited domains across ChatGPT, Google AI Mode, Gemini, Perplexity and AI Overviews. In the same period, ProFound's 680 million-citation dataset showed different platform patterns: Wikipedia accounted for 7.8 percent of ChatGPT citations, Reddit 6.6 percent of Perplexity citations, and Reddit 2.2 percent of Google AI Overviews citations.

Those percentages are not market share in the ordinary media sense. They describe citation distributions in particular datasets. The useful conclusion is narrower: source choice changes with the question and the engine.

Parse's 2026 analysis of citation sources reports YouTube, Reddit and Wikipedia near the top of the multi-platform source mix. A separate analysis reported by Everything-PR examined more than 680 million citations and found a highly concentrated distribution, with a small number of domains accounting for a large share of citations. Treat the numbers as directional, not as a universal ranking. The practical point survives both studies: source choice shapes visibility.

The Reddit problem

Reddit is useful because it contains first-hand experience, product comparisons and language that brand sites rarely publish. It is difficult because a brand cannot simply buy its way into a credible conversation.

A Reddit thread that gets cited is not a conventional media placement. It has its own author, context, comment history and moderation rules. A polished brand answer copied into a subreddit may do more harm than good. The useful work is listening first, finding questions that are already being discussed, and contributing information that can stand without a sales pitch.

The source also changes the measurement question. A brand mention in a Reddit thread is not equivalent to a link from a trade publication. Both may influence an answer, but they carry different context and different risks. Reports that collapse them into a single citation count conceal that difference.

Wikipedia and the evidence chain

Wikipedia is frequently cited because it gives models a structured, sourced account of an entity. It is also one of the least controllable places in a communications plan.

The answer is not to write promotional copy for an encyclopaedia. The answer is to make the underlying evidence easier to verify: independent coverage, clear company facts, consistent naming, and sources that can be checked by somebody who has no commercial interest in the outcome.

This is where earned media and technical work meet. Coverage that names an organisation but does not explain what it does gives a model little to work with. A factual article, a maintained company profile and a consistent entity footprint create a stronger chain for retrieval.

A different media plan

Build the plan from the source map.

  1. Run the buyer prompt set across the engines your audience uses.
  2. Record the domains cited in each answer, including community and video sources.
  3. Separate sources you can pitch from sources you can only influence indirectly.
  4. Map the factual gaps that make your brand hard to describe accurately.
  5. Track changes in source mix, not only whether the brand was mentioned.

The result is more specific than a generic PR target list. It says which sources matter for which questions, what kind of evidence each source accepts, and where the brand can participate without manufacturing a conversation.

What this does not mean

It does not mean every brand needs a Reddit strategy. It does not mean a Wikipedia page guarantees visibility. It does not mean earned media replaces a technically sound website.

Source maps are also unstable. Engines change retrieval systems, communities change, and a citation pattern in one category may not repeat in another. Re-run the map rather than treating an annual source list as permanent.

The commercial decision is simple: stop asking only where a brand publishes. Ask where the answer gets its evidence.
