The problem

Search became answers. Your SEO didn't follow.

Organic & AI Search is not rebadged SEO. The signals differ in several important ways: keyword density matters less than factual clarity; backlink volume matters less than citation frequency across authoritative sources; page speed matters less than whether your content can be parsed into a direct answer.

We have measured brands ranking first on Google that are entirely absent from the AI answer to the same query.

Search engines return a list of blue links. Answer engines generate a direct response. The useful measure is share of answer, not share of clicks. The mechanics of earning that answer are specific: factual clarity, entity structure, source authority and citation frequency all matter more than keyword density or backlink volume.

Our AEO framework

Five dimensions that affect each other.

We structure AEO around five interconnected dimensions. You cannot fix citation without addressing structure, or structure without addressing signal authority.

C1
Clarity
Does the model understand what you are saying? We restructure content so the model can extract the answer directly: one question per page, concise answers in the first paragraph, clear entity relationships marked up in schema.
C2
Authority
Models cite sources other sources cite. We map which outlets, research papers, forums, and data sets carry authority in your category, then build a prioritised plan for earning citations there.
C3
Structure
Schema, headings, entity relationships ; the scaffolding that tells a model what a page is about. Without it, even good content gets ignored because the model cannot categorise it reliably.
C4
Freshness
Models weight recent content differently. A page from 2021 is nearly invisible even if still correct. We build content programmes that keep the model's view of your category updated.
C5
Measurement
Share of answer across models. Whether you are named, at what position, in response to which prompts: tracked month over month against a fixed, published baseline.
How AEO works

Content built for extraction, not ranking.

Traditional SEO writes for the searcher. AEO writes for the model. The difference shows up in how the page is structured: each question gets its own page, the answer sits in the first paragraph, supporting detail follows, and schema marks up every entity the model needs to verify.

This is not a content-quantity play. A single page that directly answers one buyer question, with the right entity signals and source authority, can outperform dozens of pages written for keyword variation. The model does not need volume. It needs something it can cite with confidence.

The technical foundation

AI cannot cite what it cannot read.

Organic and AI search depend on the same underlying access. Search engines may render a JavaScript-heavy page in a browser, while many AI crawlers rely on the raw HTML returned by the server. If product detail, article text, pricing, or specifications only appear after interaction or client-side rendering, the content may be absent from the response an AI system receives.

01
Crawler access.
Review robots.txt and server rules so relevant AI crawlers can reach public content without exposing sensitive paths.
02
Initial HTML and rendering.
Compare the browser view with the raw response. Repair missing content caused by JavaScript-only rendering, hidden tabs, accordions, or delayed loading.
03
Schema and entity signals.
Use structured data to clarify what a page, product, organisation, or claim represents and how its entities relate.
04
Sitemaps and machine-readable guidance.
Prioritise the pages that answer buyer questions, maintain accurate sitemaps, and use llms.txt where it supports a clear route to important content.

This work is part of the search programme, not a separate service line. Technical fixes make content available and understandable. They do not create authority or make a weak proposition citable on their own.
