AI is changing digital advertising. The buying model is changing with it.

The first phase automated tasks. The next phase changes how campaigns are planned, made, bought and judged. The useful question is what belongs inside the machine, and what still needs a person.

19 SEPTEMBER 2026 · 12 MIN READ · INFLEXION JOURNAL
On this page01 / The scale is already here02 / Three phases of the change03 / Creative becomes a testing system04 / Media buying becomes continuous05 / Measurement gets harder06 / Inflexion's view07 / What advertisers should do

Digital advertising has spent two decades becoming more automated. AI is making the remaining manual work cheaper, faster and less central to the job.

The change is already visible in the numbers. Digital advertising revenue in the US reached $259 billion in 2024, up 15 percent year on year, according to the IAB and PwC. Programmatic revenue reached $134.8 billion, up 18 percent. Retail media reached $53.7 billion, up 23 percent.

Those figures describe a large and growing market. They do not prove that AI caused the growth. They do show the environment into which AI is arriving: more inventory, more platforms, more data and more pressure to explain where the money went.

01. The scale is already here

IAB Europe's 2025 AI advertising survey found that 85 percent of respondents were already using AI-based marketing tools. Nearly three quarters said at least one campaign function was AI-powered. Targeting and content generation led adoption.

The survey also showed the gap between use and control. Sixty-eight percent of companies had general internal AI guidelines, but only 43 percent had guidelines specifically covering marketing and advertising. Privacy was the leading concern. Internal expertise and training were also major barriers.

That combination matters. The industry is not deciding whether AI will enter advertising. It is deciding how much of the operating model can change before the controls catch up.

02. Three phases of the change

Phase one: assistance

AI started as a faster junior team member. It wrote first drafts, summarised reports, resized assets, generated audience ideas and answered questions about campaign data. A person still made the decision. The gain was time.

This phase is now ordinary. The useful distinction is not whether a team uses an AI tool. It is whether the output enters the work without a clear owner checking it.

Phase two: variation

Generative AI changes the economics of testing. The IAB's 2025 video advertising report found that 86 percent of buyers were using or planning to use generative AI to build video creative. Buyers expected AI-generated creative to account for 40 percent of all ads by 2026.

Buyers were using the technology to create audience versions, visual-style changes and context-specific variations. Those are practical uses. They shorten the distance between a hypothesis and a test.

Phase three: adaptation

The next phase is not simply more variants. It is a system that changes allocation, creative and audience decisions as new evidence arrives.

An agent can move a budget between campaigns. A creative system can produce a new version when a hook underperforms. A planning system can combine product, audience and outcome data before a media plan is approved.

The decision is still commercial. The system is simply able to act on it more often.

03. Creative becomes a testing system

AI makes production abundant. That creates a selection problem.

When a team can produce ten versions of a video instead of two, the work is no longer finished when the assets are exported. The team needs a clear hypothesis for each version: a different opening, proof point, product demonstration, offer or audience context.

The IAB report found that buyers were using generative AI to create versions for different audiences, visual styles and contextual settings. That is more useful than treating AI as a way to make one finished ad more cheaply. It turns creative into a set of tests.

The risk is volume without learning. A hundred variants do not make a campaign intelligent if nobody knows which variable changed or whether the result was large enough to matter.

Inflexion's view is simple: give each creative test a job. If the brief cannot say what the version is testing, it is production activity, not learning.

04. Media buying becomes continuous

Most media operations still run on a human calendar. A team plans, launches, reviews, then changes the plan in the next meeting. AI compresses the time between those decisions.

Automated bidding is not new. What changes is the number of signals that can be combined: product availability, retailer search position, conversion quality, audience response, creative fatigue, viewability, supply path and business outcomes.

The machine can respond to these signals continuously. The planning question moves upstream. Instead of asking which bid to change, the team defines the outcome, the permitted trade-offs and the conditions that require human review.

This is especially clear in CTV. IAB research found that buyers expected 47 percent of CTV inventory to be biddable, up from 34 percent the previous year. Yet more than 80 percent of digital video buyers still wanted human assistance from sell-side partners when activating CTV programmatically.

Automation is not the same as independence. Buyers want the control of programmatic systems and the judgement of a partner who understands the inventory.

05. Measurement gets harder before it gets better

AI can make a campaign look more efficient while making the explanation less clear.

More automated decisions create more possible causes for an outcome. A conversion rate changes because of the audience, the creative, the bid, the product page, the retailer algorithm, inventory, seasonality or a combination of them.

Privacy adds another constraint. The IAB and PwC's 2024 Internet Advertising Revenue Report identifies data security, interoperability and integration as continuing problems. It also reports that 70 percent of agencies, brands and publishers were not yet fully scaling AI across media planning, activation and analysis.

The measurement response is not a larger dashboard. It is a clearer chain of evidence:

  1. What decision did the system make?
  2. Which signal caused it to make that decision?
  3. What changed in the market at the same time?
  4. What outcome moved, and over what period?
  5. Could the same result have happened without the intervention?

That last question is the difficult one. Attribution describes what happened after exposure. Incrementality asks what the exposure changed.

06. Inflexion's view

We do not see AI as a replacement for media expertise. We see it as a change in where expertise is applied.

People should not spend senior time copying reports between platforms, resizing a creative file or moving a budget because a dashboard changed colour. Those tasks can be automated.

People should decide what the campaign is meant to achieve, which evidence deserves confidence, what trade-offs the system is allowed to make, and when the apparent result is too weak to act on.

That is why our media work starts with the operating model rather than the tool. We map the signals, separate the paid and organic effects, define the decision rules and then use automation where it improves the quality of the work.

The advantage is not access to a more impressive model. It is a clearer relationship between the business objective, the media action and the evidence that follows.

07. What advertisers should do now

Start with the work that is repetitive and measurable.

  1. List the campaign decisions your team makes every week.
  2. Separate decisions that need judgement from tasks that only need speed.
  3. Check the data feeding each automated recommendation.
  4. Set explicit limits for budget, brand safety, inventory and audience use.
  5. Build a creative testing plan before generating a large volume of assets.
  6. Report the intervention and the outcome together.

Do not begin by buying another platform. Begin by writing down what the system should be allowed to do and what it should never decide alone.

AI will make digital advertising more adaptive. It will also make weak objectives, incomplete data and poor measurement move faster. The advertisers who benefit will be the ones that improve the decision system, not simply the number of tools in it.
