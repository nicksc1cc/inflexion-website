Technical GEO / AI Crawlability

AI crawlers miss JavaScript-injected structured data

19 SEPTEMBER 2026 · 9 MIN READ · INFLEXION JOURNAL
On this page
01 / The barrier 02 / What crawlers see 03 / The foundation: access, rendering, structure 04 / Schema that works 05 / llms.txt: the new robots.txt 06 / What this means for your strategy

A page can be perfectly readable in Chrome and almost empty to the crawler that supplies an AI answer engine.

The barrier is not content. It is access.

Most brands assume that publishing structured data is enough. They add JSON-LD to their pages, validate it in Google's Rich Results Test, and move on. The test passes. The markup appears in the rendered DOM. The problem is that AI crawlers do not execute JavaScript the way Googlebot does.

Our research found that 69 percent of 23 major AI crawlers could not execute JavaScript. The figure is directional rather than permanent because crawler capabilities change, but the architectural risk is clear: a client-rendered page cannot assume that every retrieval system will wait for the browser.

GPTBot, PerplexityBot, ClaudeBot, and the other major AI crawlers fetch raw HTML. They do not render. They do not wait for hydration. They do not execute your React components. If your structured data lives in a client-side script tag added after hydration, or if your product schema is injected by a tag manager, or if your FAQ markup loads via a third-party widget, the crawler sees nothing.

An investigation shared by Search Engine Journal in early 2025 confirmed the gap: AI crawlers miss JavaScript-injected structured data entirely. The only reliable path is server-side rendering or static HTML.

What crawlers see

When GPTBot requests a page, it receives the initial HTML response. That is the entire document it will ever parse. No second pass. No rendering budget. No JavaScript engine.

The practical result: a product page with perfect schema in the browser DevTools may return zero structured data to an AI crawler. The same page validates in Google's tool because Googlebot renders. The AI crawler does not.

This is not a bug. It is a design choice. AI crawlers operate at massive scale across millions of domains. Rendering every page would multiply compute cost by orders of magnitude. They trade completeness for throughput.

The foundation: access, rendering, structure

Technical GEO has three layers. Skip one and the others lose force.

Test the difference, not just the page

Run the same template twice: once with JavaScript disabled and once with a rendered browser. Compare word count, headings, product attributes, canonical tags and JSON-LD. A large difference tells you what a raw crawler cannot access. The test belongs in the deployment process, not only in an annual audit.

Layer 1: Crawler access

The first check is robots.txt. Many enterprise sites block unknown bots by default. That now includes the bots that feed the answer engines your buyers use. We routinely find four to seven major AI crawlers blocked per site.

The fix is explicit allow rules for each crawler user-agent. A minimal working set:

User-agent: OAI-SearchBot
Allow: /

User-agent: PerplexityBot
Allow: /

User-agent: GPTBot
Allow: /

User-agent: ClaudeBot
Allow: /

User-agent: Claude-SearchBot
Allow: /

User-agent: PerplexityBot
Allow: /

User-agent: CCBot
Allow: /

This is a ten-minute change for anyone with file access. It is the highest-impact action in technical GEO.

Layer 2: Server-side rendering

Structured data must exist in the initial HTML. That means either server-side rendering (Next.js getServerSideProps, Remix loaders, Astro static generation) or static HTML with the markup baked in. Client-side hydration frameworks (Create React App, vanilla SPA) cannot serve AI crawlers without a rendering layer.

If a replatform is not on the roadmap, a middleware that serves pre-rendered HTML to known AI crawler user-agents is a pragmatic bridge.

Layer 3: Schema completeness

Once crawlers can reach and read the page, the schema must be complete enough to be useful. The minimum viable set for a commercial page:

  • Product or Service schema with name, description, price, availability, brand, SKU
  • Organization schema with logo, sameAs social profiles, contactPoint
  • BreadcrumbList for site hierarchy
  • Article or BlogPosting for content pages with author, datePublished, dateModified
  • FAQPage or HowTo where applicable

JSON-LD in the head is preferred. It is easier to maintain than inline microdata and is the format both Google and AI crawlers expect.

Schema that works

Schema.org vocabulary is broad. AI crawlers use a subset. The schemas that consistently appear in citations:

Product
Ecommerce and software products
Required: name, description, offers, brand, sku
Organization
Entity identity and authority signals
Required: name, url, logo, sameAs
Article
Content pages and thought leadership
Required: headline, author, datePublished

A 2024 study by Aggarwal et al. (KDD 2024, arxiv:2311.09735) found that applying GEO techniques including structured data increased visibility in generative engines by an average of 40 percent. The gain came from pages that were already crawlable but lacked machine-readable context.

The inverse is also true: pages with perfect schema that AI crawlers cannot reach see zero gain. The layers are sequential, not independent.

llms.txt: the new robots.txt

In late 2024, a proposed standard emerged: llms.txt. A plain-text file at the domain root that tells AI systems what content exists, where it lives, and how to interpret it. Think of it as a sitemap for reasoning engines.

A minimal llms.txt:

# Inflexion AI Discovery Guide
# https://nicksc1cc.github.io/inflexion-website/llms.txt

## Core Pages
- https://nicksc1cc.github.io/inflexion-website/ : Homepage
- https://nicksc1cc.github.io/inflexion-website/aeo.html : AEO services
- https://nicksc1cc.github.io/inflexion-website/technical-geo.html : Technical GEO
- https://nicksc1cc.github.io/inflexion-website/ai-visibility-analytics.html : AI Visibility Analytics

## Resources
- https://nicksc1cc.github.io/inflexion-website/sitemap.xml : Full sitemap
- https://nicksc1cc.github.io/inflexion-website/robots.txt : Crawler permissions

## Optional: Structured Data Context
- Organization: Inflexion, marketing consultancy, AI discovery and retail media
- Key topics: AEO, GEO, retail media, Amazon, AI-powered media, measurement

The file is not yet a formal standard. Adoption is voluntary. But early adopters gain a structural advantage: when a model retrieves your domain, it reads the guide first. That shapes how it interprets everything else.

What this means for your strategy

Technical GEO does not replace content strategy. It is the foundation. Without it, the best content written is content the model literally cannot read.

The audit sequence we run:

  1. Crawler access check: robots.txt, server headers, IP allowlists
  2. Rendering audit: browser vs. raw HTML diff for every template type
  3. Schema coverage: page-level validation across the crawlable site
  4. llms.txt presence and accuracy
  5. Crawler log analysis: which bots visited, what they fetched, what they missed

The output is a prioritised remediation plan. The highest-value fixes are usually access and rendering. Schema completes the picture.

The honest limitations

Technical GEO cannot fix weak positioning. If the underlying product or service is poorly defined, making it readable does not rescue the narrative. It only ensures the model sees what is there.

Models change. Retrieval systems update. The baseline should be re-run quarterly as a minimum.
