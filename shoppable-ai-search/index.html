Shoppable AI search: the battle for share of generated sentence

Dunnhumby identifies the birth of shoppable AI search where brands battle for share of generated sentence. The focus is on summarising content for AI ingestion.

19 September 2026 · 8 min read · Inflexion journal
On this page01 / The shift from shelf to sentence02 / How AI search becomes shoppable03 / Content that gets ingested04 / Strategy for the generated sentence

Retail media used to make the placement the product. A sponsored slot, a search position or an endcap put a brand in front of a shopper. AI search changes the unit of competition: the product has to make it into the answer.

Dunnhumby describes this as "share of generated sentence" in its 2025 retail tech trends. The phrase is useful because it turns a vague visibility goal into a question: when a shopper asks for a recommendation, which brands does the system name, and what evidence does it use?

When a shopper asks an AI assistant, "What running shoes work for wide feet?" the answer may name a small set of products and explain the choice. The brands in that answer have entered the shopping journey before a retailer's search results page appears. Brands left out have lost that particular consideration moment, even if they rank well for a conventional keyword.

01. The shift from shelf to sentence.

A shelf has a visible set of slots. An AI answer has a limited amount of attention and may compress a category into a few recommendations. The competition is no longer only for position. It is for inclusion, explanation and the evidence behind the mention.

On Marketing's October 2025 survey reported that 90 percent of respondents read Google AI Overviews when they are shown, with more than half doing so most of the time. That suggests the answer layer deserves measurement, but it does not make an AI Overview a direct replacement for a retail shelf.

HubSpot's State of AEO in 2026 reports that nearly half of surveyed marketers had made a business purchase decision based primarily on brand information first discovered in an AI-generated answer. It also reports higher conversion for AI referral traffic than organic search. These figures are survey and platform measures, not a universal conversion benchmark, but they point to a buyer behaviour worth testing.

02. How AI search becomes shoppable.

Shoppable AI search has three linked jobs: make the product understandable, make the recommendation defensible and make the transaction possible.

Content ingestion. Product specifications, comparison tables, review summaries, use-case guides and FAQ pages give a system material to retrieve. Clear headings and consistent attributes make the answer easier to assemble, but structure alone does not guarantee a citation.

Recommendation. The system has to connect a shopper's question to a product and a reason to choose it. Relevance, source quality, clarity and freshness can all matter, while the weighting differs by system and prompt. The practical task is to test the prompts that matter rather than assume one ranking factor controls every answer.

Transaction enablement. Target, Etsy, Walmart and Instacart have enabled shopping experiences in ChatGPT. Where checkout or a product link is available, the recommendation can move directly into a product action. The handoff, however, still depends on the retailer, platform and market supporting that flow.

That makes the measurement problem wider than citation count. Teams need to record the question, model, answer, cited source, product position and downstream action so they can separate repeatable visibility from a single response.

03. Content that gets ingested.

Different systems retrieve different sources, so these figures should be treated as directional rather than as a universal preference order.

Comparison content. A page that answers a real comparison question gives the system a usable frame: product A versus product B for a stated need. The cited rate reported in the source material is a benchmark for that study, not a guarantee for every prompt.

Documentation and resources. Technical specifications, fit guides and use-case matrices make product attributes easier to check and compare.

Informative content. Blog posts and guides can answer broader questions, but their value depends on whether they contain clear evidence and match the prompt.

Evidence. Quotes and statistics can make a page more useful when they are attributed and relevant. A number without scope or a source gives the model less reason to trust the page.

Earned media. Muck Rack's cited figure suggests that independent sources can matter when systems assemble an answer. Owned content still explains the product. Earned coverage can provide the outside corroboration that a brand page cannot provide on its own.

The practical shift is from a keyword list to a question set. Map the questions shoppers ask, answer them directly, structure the evidence clearly and distribute it through sources that buyers and answer systems already use.

04. Strategy for the generated sentence.

A workable programme starts with a fixed prompt set and a record of what each system returns.

Map the question set. Use questions such as "What running shoes work for wide feet?" or "Which television works in bright rooms?" Each question defines a different evidence requirement.

Build the answer library. Put the direct answer first, then supporting evidence. Use comparison tables, specification lists and review summaries where they make the choice easier to verify.

Earn the citations. Identify the sources that appear for your buyer questions, then build useful evidence for those publications and communities. The source map should guide outreach, not replace it.

Keep the entity consistent. Audit the descriptions, names and attributes that appear across reference sources. Inconsistencies make it harder for a system to connect the company, product and evidence.

Participate where the questions are asked. Reddit, YouTube, specialist forums and other communities can contain the language buyers use. Answer real questions without turning every reply into a product pitch.

Track the answer, not just the rank. For a fixed prompt set and model set, record whether the brand appears, where it appears, why it is recommended, which source is cited and whether the result repeats across runs.

Keep the prompts, models, dates and scoring rules stable enough to distinguish a real change from a different question or a different response.
